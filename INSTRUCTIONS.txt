RULES FOR ALL RUNS
============================================================
- Professional, plain advisory tone. No overclaims. Every figure is
  current and sourced; anything unverifiable goes in the review note,
  not the content.
- No client names or data traceable to a client.
- Write for every funding arrangement. Atria's clients include fully
  insured, level-funded, and self-funded plans, and every piece must
  be useful to all of them. Never write as if every employer makes
  plan decisions itself. Where the decision-maker differs, say so and
  give each its angle:
  - Self-funded (and level-funded, where it applies): what the plan
    sponsor decides and the levers it controls.
  - Fully insured: the carrier makes the decision, so the employer's
    job is to understand how its carrier handles the issue (coverage
    criteria, prior authorization, care management, programs offered,
    renewal impact), what to ask the carrier and Atria, and whether
    that approach fits its workforce.
  Do not split the piece into separate fully insured and self-funded
  sections. Write one story that flows, with short inline call-outs
  where the angle differs, e.g. "Fully insured? This still matters:
  ask your carrier how it ..." or "Self-funded? You set this rule, so
  ...". The tone is reassurance that the topic matters to every
  employer, whatever its funding, and here is how it applies to you.
  In the PPTX, use at most one or two compact call-outs (a sentence in
  a card body, callout item, or stat description), never a two-column
  divide.
  Example: on GLP-1s, a self-funded employer decides coverage and
  utilization rules; a fully insured employer should know its
  carrier's coverage criteria, prior authorization and step rules,
  weight-management programs, and how GLP-1 cost shows up in renewal.
- Tribal-specific content only when the Topic says so.
- Research. Use only trusted, highly reputable sources, as broadly as
  the topic needs. Government primary sources are required for every
  rule, limit, deadline, and legal requirement; the other tiers add
  data, context, and practical guidance.
  Tier 1, primary (required for rules and figures): IRS, Treasury,
  DOL/EBSA, HHS, CMS, CDC, NIH, FDA, USDA (MyPlate, FoodData Central),
  OSHA, EEOC, SSA, OPM, IHS, CISA, NIST, eCFR, Federal Register,
  Congress.gov, GAO, CBO, healthcare.gov; state agencies (California:
  DMHC, CDI, FTB, EDD, CDPH, Covered California; Minnesota and
  Wisconsin insurance and revenue departments when relevant).
  Tier 2, research and nonpartisan data: KFF, EBRI, Commonwealth Fund,
  RAND, Peterson-KFF Health System Tracker, NAIC, Bureau of Labor
  Statistics, Census, peer-reviewed journals (JAMA, NEJM, Health
  Affairs, The Lancet), Mayo Clinic, Cleveland Clinic, American Heart
  Association, American Cancer Society, Academy of Nutrition and
  Dietetics.
  Tier 3, recognized industry and legal analysis: Mercer, Milliman,
  Segal, WTW, Aon, Business Group on Health, IFEBP, SHRM, PwC Health
  Research Institute, Insurance Information Institute, NCCI, AM Best,
  Verisk/ISO, and benefits-law firm client alerts (for example Groom
  Law Group, Proskauer, Littler, Jackson Lewis); tribal: NIHB, NCAI,
  Tribal Self-Governance Advisory Committee.
  Never cite: blogs, vendor or carrier marketing, press-release
  aggregators, social media, forums, AI-generated content farms, or
  Wikipedia. A Tier 3 claim about a rule must be confirmed in Tier 1.
  Survey figures name the survey, publisher, and year.
  Find pages with WebSearch. Read each source with WebFetch; if
  WebFetch fails or is unavailable, use the kit's fetch helper from
  the repository root, which works on sites that refuse automated
  tools:
    python3 fetch.py "<url>"                 page or PDF as text
    python3 fetch.py ecfr 45 147.130         current regulation text
    python3 fetch.py fedreg "search words"   newest Federal Register items
  Cite the exact URL you read. If a source still cannot be read after
  WebFetch and fetch.py, try a second primary source for the same
  fact; only then mark it UNVERIFIED in review.txt, quoting the exact
  FETCH FAILED message.
- Never send email, publish, or post anything. This task only drafts
  and saves files.

WORDPRESS FILES (Track A posts and every Track B post)
wordpress.html holds ONLY the HTML that gets pasted into the WordPress
Code Editor: it starts with the first <p> and ends with the disclaimer
<p>. No title line, no image-URL line, no labels, no comments other
than "<!-- Floated mid-article image -->", no <html>, <head>, <body>,
or <h1>-<h3>. Same markup and style as the posts Dustin has pasted
for years (see _Templates/Atria-WordPress-HTML-Template.html).
wordpress-post.txt holds everything else WordPress needs, as plain
labeled lines:
  Title: [post title]
  Slug: [suggested-url-slug]
  Excerpt: [one or two sentences]
  Category: [Benefits 101 | Benefits Buzz | Well-Being | Risk Report | Blog]
  Featured image file: [kebab-name]-featured.png
  Featured image prompt: [a detailed art-direction brief, 60-120 words]
  Mid-article image prompt: [same standard, its own scene]
  Featured image alt text: [plain description]
  Mid-article image file: [kebab-name].png  (must match the src in
    wordpress.html: https://atriains.com/wp-content/uploads/YYYY/MM/
    [kebab-name].png, YYYY/MM = send/post date)
  Mid-article image alt text: [must match the alt in wordpress.html,
    and describe what the prompt asks for; if the prompt has no
    people, the alt text has no people]
  Topic test for both prompts: a reader who sees only the image should
    be able to guess the issue's subject (e.g., prescription bottles and
    coins for PBM rebates; a clinic waiting room for site-of-care costs).
    Generic office or desk scenes (laptops, glasses, plants, blank
    folders, handshakes) fail the test unless the topic is about them.
    No blank books, folders, or papers as props: since images can't show
    text, they read as empty and add nothing.
  Image standard for both prompts: write them so an image generator
    produces a beautiful, clean, crisp, professional image that matches
    the quality of the article. Healthcare or topic-related subject
    matter, described concretely: the setting, the objects, the
    composition and camera angle, lighting, depth of field, and a calm
    palette that sits well beside Atria's navy, teal, and green
    (whites, soft blues, natural wood, plants). Editorial photography
    or refined 3D/flat illustration, never cartoonish or stock-cliche.
    Prefer objects and settings; include people only when the topic
    truly needs them, and then never real or identifiable people (no
    faces in close-up, no celebrities). No text, numbers, logos, or
    brand names in the image. State the aspect ratio: featured 1200x762
    landscape, with the main subject in the right two-thirds of the
    frame and the left third plain or soft background (Atria's logo and
    stripe overlay, _Templates/Atria-Featured-Overlay.png, covers it);
    mid-article square, at least 800x800.
  PDF link: [PDF LINK] is in wordpress.html (Track A only); replace it
    with the uploaded PDF's media URL.

   Atria-Content-Log.txt, add one line per piece drafted in this run

7. Finish with a one-paragraph summary, the file list with OK/FAIL,
   and the link to the 1-Review folder.
